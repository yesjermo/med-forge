**Homeostasis** is the maintenance of a stable internal environment (temperature, blood glucose, blood pressure, pH, osmolality) despite outside change.

Every control loop has three parts:
1. **Sensor (receptor)**: detects the variable, e.g. thermoreceptors in the skin and hypothalamus
2. **Control centre**: compares the value with a set point, e.g. the hypothalamus
3. **Effector**: makes the correction, e.g. sweat glands or skeletal muscle (shivering)

**Negative feedback** reverses a change, bringing the variable back towards the set point. Most homeostatic loops work this way:
- Body temperature rises → sweating and skin vasodilation → heat is lost → temperature falls
- Blood glucose rises → the pancreas releases insulin → cells take up glucose → glucose falls

**Positive feedback** amplifies a change until an endpoint is reached:
- During labour, stretch of the cervix triggers oxytocin release, which strengthens contractions, which stretch the cervix further, until the baby is born
- Platelets at a wound release chemicals that recruit more platelets
