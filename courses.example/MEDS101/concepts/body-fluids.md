**Total body water (TBW)** is about 60% of body mass in a typical young adult man (a little lower in women and older adults, because fat holds less water than muscle):

$$ TBW \approx 0.6 \times \text{body mass (kg)} $$

For a 70 kg adult, TBW ≈ 42 L.

It is split between two main compartments:
- **Intracellular fluid (ICF)**: about two-thirds of TBW (≈ 28 L); rich in K⁺
- **Extracellular fluid (ECF)**: about one-third of TBW (≈ 14 L); rich in Na⁺ and Cl⁻

The ECF itself divides into:
- **Interstitial fluid**: about three-quarters of the ECF, bathing the cells
- **Plasma**: about one-quarter of the ECF (≈ 3.5 L)

Water moves freely between compartments by osmosis, so a change in ECF osmolality shifts water into or out of cells.
