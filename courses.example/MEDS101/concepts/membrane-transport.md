The plasma membrane is a phospholipid bilayer with embedded proteins. How a substance crosses it depends on its size, charge and the direction of its gradient.

**Passive transport** (no ATP needed, always down the gradient):
- **Simple diffusion**: O₂, CO₂ and other small non-polar molecules pass straight through the bilayer
- **Facilitated diffusion**: channels (e.g. K⁺ channels) or carriers (e.g. GLUT glucose transporters) let polar molecules and ions through
- **Osmosis**: water moves through the bilayer and through aquaporins

The rate of diffusion rises with the concentration difference, the membrane area and the permeability (Fick's law):

$$ J = P \, A \, (C_1 - C_2) $$

**Active transport** moves substances against their gradient:
- **Primary**: uses ATP directly. The Na⁺/K⁺-ATPase pumps 3 Na⁺ out and 2 K⁺ in per ATP
- **Secondary**: uses the Na⁺ gradient made by the pump, e.g. the SGLT1 co-transporter carries glucose into gut cells with Na⁺

> Carrier-mediated transport saturates: once every carrier is busy, adding more substrate doesn't speed it up.
