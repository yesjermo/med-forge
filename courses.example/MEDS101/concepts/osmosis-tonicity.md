**Osmosis** is the net movement of water across a semipermeable membrane from a region of lower solute concentration to one of higher solute concentration.

**Osmolarity** counts all dissolved particles per litre:

$$ \text{osmolarity} = \text{molarity} \times i $$

where $i$ is the number of particles each formula unit releases. Glucose doesn't dissociate ($i = 1$); NaCl gives Na⁺ and Cl⁻ ($i \approx 2$). So 0.15 mol/L NaCl is about 300 mOsm/L.

**Tonicity** describes what a solution does to a cell placed in it, which depends on the solutes that *can't* cross the membrane:
- **Isotonic**: no net water movement; the cell keeps its volume
- **Hypotonic**: water enters; the cell swells and a red cell may burst (haemolysis)
- **Hypertonic**: water leaves; the cell shrinks (a red cell crenates)

> A solution can be iso-osmotic but hypotonic: urea crosses membranes, so cells in iso-osmotic urea still swell.
